# House-style animation approaches

Use the following as a starting library, not a fixed identity. Choose a single primary medium for each shot and keep its palette, texture, typography, and motion coherent.

| Content type | Approach | Typical duration | Core idea |
|---|---|---:|---|
| An abstract concept that benefits from a character or metaphor | Paper-cut collage | 5–9 s | Build a small physical world from layered paper shapes. |
| Evidence, source documents, or screenshots | Dossier collage | 3–7 s | Treat real material as printed artifacts on a work surface. |
| Steps, relationships, or reasoning | Sketch notebook | 4–8 s | Draw the logic as it is narrated. |

## Paper-cut collage

Use a restrained wall color, layered torn-edge shapes, paper grain, patterned surfaces, and crisp shadows. Characters and props should share the same cut-paper language. Give each object a clear landing and weight; use background motion only when it supports the scene.

## Dossier collage

Use genuine, cleared source material as the focal point: a document, screenshot, photo, chart, or chart excerpt. Add paper edges, halftone, restrained tape, and marker annotations as needed. Do not invent evidence or make a mockup look like an authentic source. Measure annotations against rendered text bounds.

## Sketch notebook

Use a paper surface, graphite or ink linework, handwritten annotations, and one or a few accent colors. Keep characters symbolic and consistent with the chosen medium. Animate the drawing process and retain small, intentional hand-made irregularities.

Implementation details live in `sketch.md` and `sketch-styles.md`.

## Fonts and licensing

Check fonts on the rendering machine. Verify commercial-use terms before using a font in a commercial deliverable; if the terms are unclear, choose a clearly licensed alternative.
