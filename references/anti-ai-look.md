# Avoiding generic, template-like motion design

Use this checklist at style approval and after rendering. If a shot feels interchangeable with a stock template, change the visual idea—not only its colors.

## Defaults to challenge

- Reusing the same warm neutral, dark brown, and orange palette in every project.
- Unrequested neon gradients, glowing outlines, and purple-to-blue backgrounds.
- Building every frame from identical rounded cards or glass panels.
- Leaving all shapes perfectly clean when the chosen medium should show paper, ink, halftone, pencil, chalk, or pixel texture.
- Centering a generic title on a gradient, adding decorative rules, corner page numbers, chapter counters, progress bars (the opt-in chapter bar in rules L10 is the only exception), or using an automatic 50/50 split.
- Using fade-up for every entrance, typewriter reveals, jelly bounce, one easing curve throughout, or long shots where only elements appear one by one.

## Stronger choices

- Make the chosen medium legible at phone size.
- Show an idea through an action rather than attaching a label to it.
- Introduce a meaningful change every 2–4 seconds; vary shot scale and direction.
- Carry one visual motif through a sequence and let it change with the argument.
- Keep subtle motion in still scenes.
- Give each accent color one clear job.
- Give objects weight and contact; choose motion curves appropriate to the material.
